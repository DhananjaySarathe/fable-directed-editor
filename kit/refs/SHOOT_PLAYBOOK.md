# Shoot playbook: making the next story film better

Written 2026-10-26 after editing films/not-ai-made. It covers what the footage made hard in the edit, how to plan and record the next one, and what to watch. It applies to any "Netflix-style" format: documentary, mockumentary, mini-series, short film, debate, or explainer with a story.

## 1. What this film taught us (from the edit)

| What happened | What it cost in the edit | Next time |
|---|---|---|
| **Little footage:** 13 takes, 5–17 s each, about 2.5 min raw for a 4:20 film | Boards, explainers and B-roll had to carry most of the film. Few choices of delivery, almost no reactions | Shoot 4–5× the final length. Every line 2–3 times (calm / stronger / a beat of silence before and after), plus a reaction pass (below) |
| **Faces small in frame:** the Fresher's face was ~190 px wide in 4K (5% of the frame) | Punch-ins and thumbnails go soft; I had to hunt the takes frame by frame for a usable face | Frame chest-up, face 12–15% of the frame width, headroom kept. Or add a second phone at 45° for a tight angle |
| **Bright, flat, different rooms:** a white room with a carrom board, a window room, a white wall with a green shadow behind the Manager | Every room had to be darkened in post; halos around hair; the Manager needed a colour fix. Not a Netflix look out of camera | Low-key light (section 3): one big soft light at 45°, background 2–3 stops darker, overheads off, curtains closed, a lamp in the back. No white walls behind the face |
| **Few strong expressions** | The best faces (surprise, "ugh", unimpressed, laugh) were 0.3-s moments mid-sentence | A 2-minute reaction pass per character, done on purpose |
| **One B-roll clip** (the café), plus screen recordings | Repeated board shots; the story had nothing physical to show | A B-roll list per scene (section 4), and real props: printouts, a real red-thread board, sticky notes |
| **Mixed eyelines:** some lines to the lens, some off it | Doc and confessional styles mixed by accident | Decide per format: documentary interview = just off the lens; mockumentary confessional = into the lens (section 3) |
| **The script was written around the takes, not before them** (his words: "the script will be better from the next film") | Jokes and beats were found in the edit, not written | A beat sheet and a shot list before the shoot (section 2) |

What worked, so keep it:
- Three characters by wardrobe and room.
- The red-thread case board.
- Real logs and screenshots as evidence.
- A scored soundtrack with drops on story moments.
- The 4K30 takes.

## 2. Plan before you shoot (one evening)

1. **One sentence.** "Can AI build a website that doesn't look AI-made?" If it doesn't fit a sentence, the film isn't clear yet.
2. **The beat sheet**, on one page (Dan Harmon's story circle works for any format):
   - **Cold open (0–30 s):** the best or strangest moment of the film, shown out of order. Then the question.
   - **The want:** what each character wants and why it's hard (the Fresher wants it fast, the Senior wants it right, the Manager wants it shipped).
   - **Three attempts, each higher stakes:** try → fail → learn → try again.
   - **The twist:** the thing nobody expected (here: both sites are AI).
   - **The payoff:** the answer, shown, not told.
   - **The button:** a last line or joke after the payoff.
3. **Write talking heads as short answers to a question.** One idea each, 1–3 sentences, ending on a "button" (a last line with a pause after it). Comedy lives in the pause and the look.
4. **Read it aloud, timed.** About 130–150 words a minute. Cut every line that only explains.
5. **The shot list:** one row per beat, with these columns:
   - A-roll (who says what);
   - B-roll;
   - screen capture;
   - insert or prop;
   - reaction;
   - music cue.
6. **Hindi or Hinglish characters:** write their lines in the script exactly as spoken, with the English subtitle beside each. Whisper writes Hindi in Devanagari and drops words, so the script is what the subtitles are made from. Keep Hindi lines short and natural, not translated English.
   - A good fit: a Hindi-speaking client, dad, uncle or investor as the outside voice, like TVF's "Tech Conversations With Dad".

## 3. Recording

**Light (the Netflix look out of camera):**
- One big soft light 45° to the side, a little above the eyes.
- The far side of the face falls into shadow.
- The background is 2–3 stops darker than the face.
- One practical (a lamp, a monitor glow) in the background, out of focus.
- Overheads off. Never a bright white wall behind the face.
- Each character gets their own dark set and one colour accent: say the Senior warm, the Fresher cool, the Manager neutral.

**Eyeline:**
- **Documentary interview:** look at an "interviewer" just beside the lens (a person or a ball taped on a stand). Keep that side the same for each character all film.
- **Mockumentary confessional:** talk into the lens. The silent look to the lens after a line is the joke.
- **Debate:** two characters face each other at three-quarter, both off the lens.

**Framing and coverage:**
- Chest-up, eyes on the upper third line, the face about 12–15% of the frame width.
- Shoot every scene twice: once medium, once tight (or a second phone at 45°).
- Lock exposure and white balance. Same time of day for all takes of a character.

**The reaction pass,** 2 minutes per character, in costume, before you change:
- skeptical, impressed, cringe ("ugh"), laugh, eye-roll;
- staring at the screen, typing focused, a slow nod;
- silent "really?" to the lens;
- 5 seconds of saying nothing.

These carry the cuts, the comedy beats and the thumbnails.

**Sound:**
- A lav, at the same gain every take. Fan and AC off.
- 30 seconds of room tone in each room.
- A clap at the start if there are two cameras.

**Takes:** start each take two seconds before the line and hold two seconds after it. Edges in quiet make clean cuts.

## 4. B-roll and inserts, per scene

- Hands on the keyboard.
- The screen shot from an angle with the phone (not only screen recordings).
- The cursor clicking the key thing.
- Printouts going up on the board, the red thread being pulled.
- Coffee, a door, walking into the room.
- A detail of each character: the Fresher's headphones, the Senior's mug, the Manager's glasses.
- Shoot 5–10 s of each, steady, two framings.

## 5. Formats beyond the documentary

These suit an AI/dev channel and reuse the same kit:
- **Mini-series:**
  - Episodes of 6–10 min, one experiment each, ending on a cliffhanger (the next test, a twist result).
  - A recurring cold open and title card.
- **Debate:**
  - Two of the characters argue one question, e.g. "Should a fresher still learn to code?" The third moderates.
  - Rounds, a live scoreboard and split screen, with a verdict at the end.
  - Jubilee's "Middle Ground" is the format study.
- **Explainer with a story (investigation):**
  - A narrator voice-over over evidence, with the characters as witnesses.
  - Coffeezilla and Johnny Harris pacing.
- **Short film:**
  - A scripted 5–8 min story with a twist.
  - Same lighting rules, more B-roll, fewer talking heads.
